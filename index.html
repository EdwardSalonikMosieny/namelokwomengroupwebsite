About the group

The Heart of Women-Led Resilience

Namelok Women Self-Help Group brings together indigenous Maasai women from Mukogodo East and Mukogodo West Wards to reduce vulnerability and create nature-friendly livelihoods in Laikipia North drylands.

The group organizes around 200 women through beekeeping, beadwork, land restoration, invasive species control, and support for girls and young women during school sessions.

200+ women organized
2 wards reached

The challenge

Climate pressure is changing daily life.

Laikipia County faces a poverty rate of about 57%, compared with about 44% nationally. In Laikipia North, frequent droughts, COVID-19 economic impacts, and livestock losses have deepened risks for pastoralist households.

☀

Climate change

Unpredictable seasons and harsher dry periods reduce reliable household income.

⌁

Drought

Water scarcity weakens livelihoods, food security, and community wellbeing.

◇

Livestock losses

Many families have lost animals that once supported income and nutrition.

%

Poverty

Economic shocks make school fees, food, and health needs harder to meet.

◒

Food insecurity

Reduced livestock and income affect meals, children, and family stability.

♀

Women and girls

Women and girls carry disproportionate burdens during crisis and drought.

Programs

Practical livelihoods rooted in culture and land.

✺

Beekeeping

Honey production creates climate-friendly income while supporting pollination and dryland biodiversity.

◆

Beadwork

Traditional Maasai beadwork strengthens women's enterprise, identity, and market access.

♧

Land restoration

Women participate in restoring degraded land, improving soil health, and protecting shared resources.

✕

Invasive species control

Community action reduces invasive plants that threaten grazing areas and native ecosystems.

₿

Women livelihood empowerment

Collective training and savings-minded activities improve household resilience and dignity.

●

School girls and menstrual dignity

The group supports girls and young women facing difficulties during school sessions.

Impact

Organized women. Stronger families. Healthier drylands.
